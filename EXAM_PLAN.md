## Oral interaction must be a core feature

The candidate guide specifies paired examination: an examiner interview (2 minutes per candidate), interaction between candidates (4 minutes per pair, without preparation time), and individual production (3 minutes per candidate). Its example production task asks candidates to choose two images, discuss them, and connect the subject to personal preferences with reasons.

Product implications: create separate interview, partner-dialogue, and image-prompt modes. Build short scaffolded versions first, then the published task durations. In dialogue, practice asking questions, responding, suggesting alternatives, and inviting the partner to speak. An AI partner must leave conversational space and respond to what the learner actually says. A teacher or another human remains useful for checking transfer to real interaction.

Source: [oral candidate guide](https://roexam.com/prezentare-proba-orala-b1/).

## Feedback should reflect the published evaluations

Writing uses four criteria: communicative effectiveness, language complexity, organization, and accuracy. Each task has up to 2.5 points per criterion. Both tasks are required. Examiner commentary rewards relevant, developed responses with coherent connections; weaknesses include unclear time reference, insufficient argument, and confusion in vocabulary.

App design: provide feedback in those four categories, supported by specific passages from the answer. Show the original attempt, a focused revision, and an opportunity to retry. Word count alone does not establish task completion. Any automated score is an estimate until checked against human judgment.

Source: [writing evaluations](https://roexam.com/exemple-evaluare-scriere-b1/).

Speaking uses communicative effectiveness, language complexity, fluency/coherence, and accuracy. The evaluation examples group interview plus interaction separately from individual production. Comments identify problems such as drifting away from the assigned task and monopolizing the dialogue. Strong examples can still have grammar errors or first-language influence on pronunciation.

App design: reward relevance, reasons, intelligibility, and reciprocal interaction. Do not equate accent with failure, uninterrupted speech with good dialogue, or a clean transcript with strong speaking. Retain audio for self-comparison when the learner chooses to save it.

Source: [speaking evaluations](https://roexam.com/exemple-evaluare-vorbire-b1/).

## Coverage beyond the current notes

The preparation page specifies writing task A at 70–80 words and task B at 90–100 words. Tasks include correspondence and short narrative, presentation, or opinion texts. Listed grammar extends to past forms, conditionals, pronouns, passive constructions, and logical connectors; topics span everyday services, education, work, travel, and social situations.

Build a coverage matrix with “not assessed,” “learning,” “supported success,” and “independent success.” Diagnose prerequisites before adding advanced forms. Current course labels alone do not tell us which gaps the learner has. Let the teacher set priorities, while ensuring the app eventually samples the whole published syllabus.

### Adaptive session length

| Available time | Complete session | Intended result |
|---|---|---|
| 5 minutes | About 1 minute of due recall, 3 minutes on one small task, 1 minute of feedback/retry | Maintain recall and practice one useful action |
| 15 minutes | Five-minute core plus a longer listening, reading, writing, or speaking task and feedback | Develop connected understanding or production |
| 30 minutes | Core plus substantial task practice, review, and a changed scenario | Build independence and transfer |

The allocations are flexible estimates. Offer a default “Start 5 minutes” and quick 15/30-minute choices. After the core, offer one clearly scoped extension such as “Practice the conversation · about 10 min.” Let the learner change the time budget. Finish naturally at task boundaries; preserve unfinished practice work. Do not add more due reviews just because a longer budget is selected.

Choose one main skill per short session, rotating coverage over the week and prioritizing observed weaknesses. Quiet mode can substitute a suitable text task while rescheduling oral work. Missing a day does not create debt. Five minutes completes the daily commitment; rewards for additional work remain separate from consistency and demonstrated mastery.

Within 30 minutes, a learner can practice the published listening duration or oral task sequence, or complete an individual writing task. The full writing component is 50 minutes and reading and grammar/vocabulary are each listed as 45 minutes. Splitting those across days is useful practice but must not be labeled a full timed simulation. Longer mock sessions can be coordinated with teacher lessons if feasible; they are not part of the confirmed daily budget.

## First mission storyboard: solve a housing problem

An original scenario connected to the home vocabulary in lesson 12:

1. Recall useful home words and a few phrases for describing a problem.
2. Listen to a short landlord message and identify what action is proposed.
3. Write a short request describing the problem and proposing a solution. Early attempts can be shorter; progress to the task-A word range.
4. Role-play a discussion about a repair appointment. The partner proposes an inconvenient time; negotiate another.
5. Receive one task-completion observation and one language correction. Retry the difficult turn.
6. Earn a mission reward; schedule related recall for another day and a changed situation later.

Scaffolds introduce missing language before requiring independent use. A later image task can compare housing situations and explain a preference. This is an authored practice scenario, not an official sample item.

Gamification remains integral: finite missions, topic choice, a lightweight journey, and rewards for completion, correction, and delayed recall. Exam mode ends with feedback; it does not interrupt timed work with hints or celebrations. Keep activity rewards separate from demonstrated exam skills.
